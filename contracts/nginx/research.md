Current TLS mandatory algorithms/extensions/KeyUpdate/errata are mapped in [shared research](../shared/tls/research.md), superseding the initial TLS-R1 placeholder. Full comparator/source/native audits remain explicitly unreviewed and are not claimed. Final nginx census has 76 core/platform and 381 adjacent dispositions after preserving a keyword-only vendor nginx analogue; product identity f5:nginx_open_source adds 26 already known records and no new identifier. These dispositions complete the declared bounded discovery scope, not immunity or implementation assurance.

Final version-control challenge WEB-R3: RFC 9110 sections 2.5/15.6.6 distinguish major protocol refusal from a higher minor version. A generic 505 for every non-1.1 token was inaccurate. The conditional profile now processes higher HTTP/1 minor versions using 1.1 semantics, refuses otherwise valid HTTP/1.0 by explicit local 403/close policy, and reserves 505 for unsupported major versions. A-NGX-02 verifies syntax, version, no origin effects and a valid 1.1 neighbor separately. This is a researched profile clarification, not additional accepted compatibility scope or a runtime result.

Final WEB-R4 errata and response/identity review: the RFC9110 catalog has verified7138(media quality illustration),7306(optional whitespace after range equals),7419(charset field name),7105(no Common Name reference identity),7109(308 method preservation),8268(absolute-form change note relocation). Selected static profile does not negotiate content variants; proxy preserves permitted end-to-end fields and never follows redirects. NGX-05/A-NGX-05 explicitly supports range whitespace; TLS-03/NGX-09/A-NGX-09 has no CN fallback. Reported9162/9164 concern field-join spacing/normalized collected grammar and are not treated as verified wire changes; ordered list combination preserves syntax. Rejected7107,7599,7870,8138,8459,7530 are retained as rejected, not alternative framing/status/redirect definitions. In particular valid status is100–599 and205 has zero content; A-NGX-07 covers these with valid neighbors. RFC9112 verified7744/8284 and rejected7214/7633 preserve obs-text reference, absolute-form precedence, case-insensitive ABNF hex and strict chunk line framing. RFC9931 catalog returned no matching errata. Sources: [RFC9110 errata](https://errata.rfc-editor.org/search/?rfc_number=9110&presentation=records), [RFC9112 errata](https://errata.rfc-editor.org/search/?rfc_number=9112&presentation=records), [RFC9931 errata](https://errata.rfc-editor.org/search/?rfc_number=9931&presentation=records).

HTTPS context challenge: a configured SNI identity for one authority must not silently authorize a different routed authority because both are operator-configured somewhere. The original draft's generic binding phrase was underspecified. NGX-09/A-NGX-09 now names finite per-identity authority mapping, explicit default-or-refusal for missing SNI, failure for unknown SNI and421/no-origin-effect on unauthorized effective authority. Absolute-form authority still governs. These are explicit unaccepted product-profile details, not new user answers. ## Approaches and capabilities

### Retained approach comparison

Revision: nginx-design-r2. Nonnormative, conditional on scope decisions. No measurements. All three approaches must meet the same [draft public contract](nginx.md), permitted foundation, exact origin authority and finite resource/consistency gates. Criteria declared before ranking: security reviewability, authority and failure ownership, plausibility under every efficiency/resource gate, independent testability, compatible reuse, maintenance effort and independence of design choices. Ineligible choices cannot win by anticipated speed.

| Approach | Consequential high-level choice | Authority, cancellation, resources and review burden | Screen and rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Complete exchange admission | Resolve each bounded request and origin response completely before committing its response representation to a client. | Broadest ability to emit a clean precommit error; request and response storage charges may reduce useful concurrency. Cancellation before origin commitment has no origin effect; after commitment cannot promise rollback. Needs admission evidence for maximum legal bodies and replies. Shared crypto/TLS/stream obligations remain owned. | Conditionally eligible; preferred for reviewability and fewer partial-response states. Performance and large-body capacity are unproven. |
| Incremental exchange commitment | Permit origin request/response progress before their whole messages are available, within the same fixed authority and bounds. | Lower expected retained content, earlier output; mid-message errors require detectable truncation and connection closure. More partially committed interactions, unread-output and origin-side effects to verify. | Conditionally eligible; second. More failure-ordering obligations, potential memory benefit is a hypothesis. |
| Size-dependent commitment | Treat bounded short exchanges as complete units and permit incremental delivery for larger exchanges at an operator-visible declared size boundary while keeping one public semantics. | May combine bounded short-work admission with lower retention for long work; crossing the boundary during unknown-length delivery requires valid contract-preserving handling. Both modes and their boundary must be independently verified. | Conditionally eligible; third due to two modes and boundary assumptions. No selected threshold or implementation layout is mandated. |

Pairwise distinction: complete versus incremental changes whether a response can be committed before its end is known, trading retained bytes for truncation states. Complete versus size-dependent changes whether large exchanges enter a distinct commitment regime, increasing boundary verification. Incremental versus size-dependent changes whether short exchanges receive an all-before-commit guarantee as a design choice, adding a transition and potentially different admission pressure. One ordinary implementation cannot make all three incompatible commitment choices for the same exchange; confinement, authentication and bounds are obligations of all three and do not count as alternatives. The chosen contract permits each; the design preference adds no normative all-or-nothing proxy guarantee.

Revisit preference if full-envelope efficiency or legal-maximum admission fails, if publication assumptions change, or if independent evaluation exposes an unmanageable commitment state. Required crypto cannot be delegated to OpenSSL, a helper or a kernel crypto provider in any option.
