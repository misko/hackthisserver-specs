# Standalone Redis-compatible public contract

Identity: redis-r2. Document agreement: Draft. Every R and R-CMD requirement has implementation evidence Untested. Owner: future Redis maintainer; product choices: user. This fresh proposed profile preserves accepted RESP2/RESP3 standalone scope and the recipe's five types, expiry, transactions, pub/sub, authentication, TLS and persistence. It does not preserve an unidentified historical deployment. [Catalog](catalog.md) defines 60 proposed commands; [acceptance](acceptance.md), [tasks](acceptance.md#future-tasks), [research](research.md), [census](security.md#core-original-record-disposition-census) and [decisions](../decisions.md) identify dependencies and blockers. Requirements R-01 through R-16 map to A-R with the same number; catalog requirements map to A-CMD with the same number.

Intended users are operator-authorized application clients, with binary-safe keys and bounded datasets, not anonymous remote administrators. Proposed platform is Linux x86-64 ELF and a generic dedicated resource envelope. Wire command/response compatibility is claimed only for the finite catalog and explicit option subsets in both accepted RESP versions. Configuration, CLI syntax, modules and upstream storage files are excluded. Proposed reference Redis Open Source 8.10.1 is pinned to 3399357e7c17b668289386b8a15a3037bc4527b1 with equally durable acknowledgement and TLS settings for comparisons; no reference has been built or run.

## Scope, assumptions and finite profiles

Required types: strings, lists, hashes, sets and sorted sets. Required mechanisms: expiry including absolute times, atomic multi-key commands, MULTI/EXEC/DISCARD/WATCH/UNWATCH, bounded blocking pops, exact-channel pub/sub, default-account credential authentication, RESP2/RESP3 negotiation, TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3, persistent mutation acknowledgement and restart recovery. The default-account authority and exact-channel restriction remain proposed product policies, not accepted portions inferred from the user's broader bundle.

Excluded commands in the catalog get ERR without key/file/network/execution effects. No replication, cluster, Sentinel, script/function engine, dynamic module, remote administration, arbitrary file path, RDB/AOF compatibility or unauthenticated data access is exposed. No remote command may configure a credential, durable mode or storage destination. No TLS client certificate is implicitly a Redis account. TLS-only service is the proposed operational default; plaintext is permitted solely in an explicit isolated functional/reference profile under operator control, never by TLS negotiation fallback.

Operators, configured credential material and storage namespace are trusted; remote clients may be hostile even when authorized for ordinary catalog data. The selected account has all catalog key/channel authority and no server administration. Malicious root/kernel, arbitrary post-ack disk corruption and storage hardware lying about durable completion are outside the proposed crash model; detected corruption or unverifiable recovery causes diagnosed unready state, never false successful recovery. Trusted clock is Unix time and must not regress below the recorded recovery time boundary; otherwise mutations stay unavailable with bounded errors until a valid operator correction. User agreement to these durability/clock/authority assumptions is pending.

KiB = 1024 bytes, MiB = 1048576 bytes. Limits apply to the complete service across listeners; a transport changes states without double-counting it. Content, encoded command bytes, retained bytes and attributable OS costs are distinct. Limits must be positive and finite; invalid combinations reject atomically before readiness/reload.

All profiles: key/field/member/channel name at most 1024 bytes; blob value at most 1 MiB; command at most 128 arguments and 4 MiB complete RESP framing; ordinary command reply at most 4 MiB including all RESP framing, except EXEC at most 65 MiB. Collection at most 1024 elements and at most 4 MiB in its largest supported serialized full reply, including field/member/score framing. Per connection: 16 outstanding pipelined commands, 16 queued transaction commands, 4 MiB total queued transaction input, 32 watched keys, 64 subscribed channels, 128 MiB pending ordinary output. Entire service pending ordinary output at most 512 MiB, pub/sub retained output at most 64 MiB; subscriber output at most 8 MiB; unauthenticated retained input/output each at most 16 KiB, 16 arguments and credential at most 4096 bytes. Diagnostic retention at most 16 MiB. Persistent storage cap is 1 GiB in F and 8 GiB in M/P, including retained recovery history and temporary replacement state; no space may be silently assumed available outside it. Finite resource accounting includes metadata, pending admission, canceled waits and cleanup; a value length alone is not a memory bound.

The maximum bulk reply is 1048588 bytes for one 1 MiB blob, including its standard length marker and terminator. A maximum full collection reply is already capped at 4 MiB including framing. Sixteen full collection replies plus the EXEC aggregate framing require at most 64 MiB plus 7 bytes, less than a 65 MiB legal EXEC-reply allowance and 128 MiB connection output cap. F admits at least one such legal transaction and dataset under the 1 GiB residency ceiling; it need not admit sixteen simultaneous maximum transactions. The aggregate 512 MiB output budget controls combined admission before mutation, rather than pretending all 64 connection maxima coexist. MSET's 4 MiB encoded-command cap overrides individually legal large values; it must reject before any update. TLS handshake/framing overhead is separately counted by tls-r3 and must fit the host/connection envelope.

Combined-reply precedence is explicit: a small MGET naming four maximum blobs would require 4194356 bytes including its array header, 52 bytes above the ordinary 4 MiB reply cap. It receives a bounded OOM reply before effects rather than a truncated array; three such blobs fit at 3145768 bytes. Individual 1 MiB GET remains supported. The same admission rule covers counted pops, many keys and transaction projections. Per-value/argument maxima do not promise every combined reply is admitted. A queue error caused by projected output dirties MULTI; if projection is only knowable at EXEC, abort before transaction effects. This is a declared original resource policy and compatibility difference requiring agreement.

Deadline starts: transport admission to completed TLS five seconds; TLS completion to successful authentication five seconds (plaintext isolated profile starts this clock at transport admission); first command byte to complete frame five seconds; complete ordinary command to complete reply two seconds including durable commitment; any stalled output five seconds; active subscription confirmation two seconds. Blocking pop timeout must be positive and at most 60 seconds; normal pop completes within two seconds after eligible input arrives and wins the defined order. WATCH and transaction lifetime 60 seconds from first WATCH or MULTI respectively, never silently renewed by tiny traffic. Authenticated idle300 seconds starts at first authentication success and renews only on completed valid useful commands; total connection lifetime1hourstarts at that first success and is never extended by reAUTH, HELLO, activity or reload; subscription with no messages counts idle and may close explicitly. Shutdown drain 65 seconds; restart readiness 30 seconds for the maximum selected data/storage envelope; overload release to ordinary authenticated control within five seconds. Larger workload lifetime is a new policy choice, not silently inherited history.

## Requirements

For every row start from a valid selected profile with controlled binary marker data and independent authenticated/unauthenticated clients. Exercise below/exact/above and cumulative limits, fragmentation/coalescing, malformed variants, interleaved clients and a valid neighbor. Observe exact response type/order, state through an independent authorized reader, unauthorized marker absence, storage/egress effects and outside continuity/resources. Default ordinary deadline is two seconds after complete framing, with five-second recovery after release unless a row defines its specific deadline. These common fields apply to all R-CMD rows as well.

### Continuing delivery cleanup profile

Under stream-r2, definitively abandoned delivery/wait work keeps its original finite connection, active/queue, byte, storage and host charges until independent observation establishes release. Missing the promised5snormal release/recovery is a failed absolute gate, not permission to erase charges. A pending item occupies its original admission allowance; replacement work must fit the remaining unchanged limits, so repeated failures cannot create unlimited hidden work. Report pending identity/cause(or unknown), current charges, owner and next due observation through the bounded diagnostic interface. No automatic restart, host replacement or new authority clears it.

Schedule one observation each 1 s through 30 s, then each 5 s while pending; each attempt≤100mswall,10msCPU,2KiB log contribution within 16MiB retained diagnostics. One bounded pending record≤16 KiB per existing charged connection/work item, never beyond original connection/active/queue caps; records are part of existing resident envelope, not extra admission. Authorized cancellation/close requests occur at definitive abandonment,2 s,5 s, then once each 60 s while needed/authorized. Missed/over-budget attempts are visible failures/unknown observations. Continue responsibility until observed reclamation or explicit accepted handover; no promised eventual completion without platform progress.

Normal application completion, closed handles and full attributable OS reclamation are separate observations. Passive kernel transport/cache retention may remain after application release; independently report its bytes/state/lifetime and keep it charged to the finite host envelope/admission accounting, never label unknown active I/O as passive or claim full reclamation solely from process residency. An unavailable OS observer leaves that assurance Inconclusive. Whole-host loss of progress cannot promise a timed on-host report; an outside observer records it, with no backdated success. These are clarified delivery-accounting duties, not an SSH account/job policy.
