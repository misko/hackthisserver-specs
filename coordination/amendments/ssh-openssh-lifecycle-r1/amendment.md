# SSH session lifecycle aligned with OpenSSH

Revision ssh-openssh-lifecycle-r1, 2026-11-04. Status: adopted requirement change. This public excerpt does not report implementation or qualification results.

## Authority and precedence

The user explicitly instructed: “Lets relax that to what openSSHD requires”, in response to the distinction between OpenSSH session cleanup and this project's stronger descendant/handle/active-I/O reclamation prerequisite. This adopts OpenSSH-style execution and session lifecycle for our existing public-key command/terminal profile. It does not adopt all OpenSSH features, replace the independently authored implementation with OpenSSH, or approve unrelated timing or dependency changes.

This amendment is the maintained effective SSH lifecycle requirement. The original contracts remain frozen historical inputs. Where they conflict, this amendment supersedes sshd-r2 S-OS-01, the S-07 cleanup profile, the lifecycle portions of S-04/10/11/12, their acceptance/task/qualification rows, and the SSH-specific application of resource-lifecycle-r1 and stream-r2. It also supersedes older instructions to preserve those stronger SSH guarantees or to await approval of this particular relaxation. Unchanged requirements retain their existing authority. A historical failure remains a failure of its original artifact and requirement revision; it is not retroactively marked passed.

## Effective execution and cleanup contract

1. **Account authority and authentication.** Authenticate the selected account by the existing public-key policy before execution. Execute with that account's intended OS identity, groups and permissions; preserve host-key/daemon-secret protection, safe inherited descriptors, authorization-file integrity, protocol denials and ordinary account filesystem/network/program access. Do not require a special all-descendant containment domain or complete kernel-operation observer merely to offer SSH service. Actual inability to establish required identity or protect secrets remains a readiness failure with a specific reason.
2. **Session completion.** Preserve exact stdin/stdout/stderr and exit status or signal where available, PTY operation and resize, and SSH EOF/channel-close ordering. EOF is directional; it need not terminate outgoing data. Observe and reap the actual direct child when it exits, close server-owned pipe/PTY/channel descriptors when their lifecycle ends, and release associated server buffers and metadata. Never report a fabricated exit status, child wait or resource release. Channel termination and child exit are distinct events; closing a channel need not await child death.
3. **Disconnect, cancellation and timeout.** Close the affected SSH transport/channel and its server-owned I/O according to the existing protocol and session deadlines. Apply ordinary authorized terminal/process signalling and normal OS session behavior as appropriate; no mandatory whole-descendant kill protocol, scheduled global reclamation audit or proof of zero outstanding account I/O is required. There is no guarantee that all user processes terminate when the channel or connection ends. A surviving ordinary account process is not automatically an SSH cleanup failure.
4. **Capacity and subsequent work.** Keep the selected one-authenticated-transport/one-live-session-channel capacity policy. Reuse session capacity when the old SSH session/channel and relevant server-owned resources are released. Do not wait for all descendants, all account-owned handles, outstanding kernel I/O or passive cache/socket allocations to disappear. Previously authorized detached/background processes may coexist with a later SSH session under ordinary account/OS policy. They remain account workloads, not new SSH channels or proof of a leaked server grant. A direct child whose status remains pending must be tracked truthfully with bounded server bookkeeping; actual exhaustion of that bookkeeping may refuse admission, but cannot justify reinstating a blanket all-descendant observer prerequisite.
5. **Responsibility boundary.** SSHD remains responsible for its own memory, descriptors, tasks, channels, buffered output, child-reaping obligations and outstanding server operations. Do not reuse a buffer or descriptor while an old server operation can still access it. The OS/account administrator is responsible for ordinary account workloads, persistent files and OS resource management after session release. Session closure is not rollback of committed filesystem/network effects, guaranteed job termination, storage reclamation, or whole-host quiescence. Do not mislabel surviving workloads as passive or claim their use is zero; simply report them outside the revised session-reclamation guarantee.
6. **Limits and platform qualification.** Preserve daemon/protocol limits, bounded retained state, ordinary inherited process limits where applicable, and protection against unbounded server-owned pending records. The old mandatory per-job, all-descendant enforcement/observation and finite passive-domain handover machinery are no longer SSH startup or replacement-session prerequisites. Account storage quotas, aggregate descendant/host measurements and environment resource envelopes may remain operator/test-environment controls and qualification measurements; they are not universal OpenSSH session-cleanup guarantees and their absence alone cannot recreate this removed startup barrier. Report measurement coverage and limitations honestly. Unrelated service resource requirements and comparative efficiency gates remain unchanged; comparisons must use equivalent revised SSH workload/account conditions.
7. **Removed stronger cleanup policy.** Withdraw the SSH-specific one-second whole-workload termination promise, 99-of-100 descendant/handle/I/O reclamation by five seconds, all-normal reclamation by thirty seconds, fixed continuing observation/retry schedule, and single pending-workload grant that blocks replacement until total reclamation. The existing shutdown admission/drain reporting deadline still concerns SSHD-owned state; it does not require all ordinary account programs or kernel activity to disappear. Report unresolved server-owned cleanup honestly and retain bounded ownership. This amendment does not introduce hard real-time kernel progress guarantees.

## Requirement and acceptance migration

| Affected identifier | Revised acceptance obligation |
| --- | --- |
| S-OS-01 / readiness | Verify the actual selected account identity, intended permissions, key/authorization protection and safe process launch. No readiness requirement for complete descendant membership, zero active I/O, a kernel-owner ledger, VM boundary, initial-user-namespace privilege as an end in itself, or a persistent passive-domain owner. Existing mechanisms may be reused if useful; they are not mandatory behavior. |
| S-04 / A-S-04 | Enforce the existing concurrent channel limit; after real channel/session release permit another useful session without requiring account-wide quiescence. Preserve exact channel identities and no cross-channel data. |
| S-05/06 / A-S-05/06 | Real authorized exec with exact input/output/error/status; real shell/PTY modes, input and resize; observe actual direct-child exit when the test requires it. No mocks or reference-only results count as candidate success. |
| S-07 / A-S-07 | Test EOF with final output, ordinary child exit and reaping, PTY close, disconnect and timeout handling, bounded server-resource release, and subsequent useful login/command. Test ordinary surviving background activity as an allowed outcome and verify that it cannot use released SSH descriptors or contaminate the next session. The evaluator separately owns bounded teardown of its own harmless background fixture. No requirement to prove zero account-wide handles/I/O. |
| S-10/11/12 and shared SSH profile | Readiness, shutdown, overload/recovery and resource evidence use the revised session/server boundary. Actual pending server operations remain accounted; detached account activity alone does not permanently consume an SSH session slot. Preserve finite server limits, legitimate recovery and exact byte delivery. |
| SSH task plans and qualification | Replace the old universal cleanup cohort and active-zero oracle with the revised cases above. Retain applicable functional, authentication, crypto, fuzz, provenance and matched performance gates. Do not substitute an unbounded test or inherited old PASS for revised evidence. |

The first executable suite must cover normal candidate startup, authorized command with exact stdout/stderr and status, PTY input/resize, unauthorized-key rejection without command execution, ordinary exit/reaping/resource release followed by a second command, and disconnect followed by another usable session. Add a bounded surviving-background case to establish the revised replacement behavior. Negative controls must catch wrong account, unauthorized execution, wrong output/status, stale session identity, unreaped exited direct children, old server I/O crossing into a new channel, and genuine server resource leaks. An observer must not require fields that this amendment expressly removes or fill them with fake zeros.

## Primary-source rationale

OpenSSH's [ChannelTimeout documentation](https://man.openbsd.org/sshd_config#ChannelTimeout) states that session termination does not guarantee removal of all associated resources and that processes may continue; an expired channel does not inherently prevent opening another channel. Its [server loop](https://github.com/openssh/openssh-portable/blob/master/serverloop.c) and [session lifecycle](https://github.com/openssh/openssh-portable/blob/master/session.c) distinguish child exit collection, exit reporting, PTY cleanup and channel closure. These are behavioral references inspected on 2026-11-04, not imported implementation code or a claim of full OpenSSH feature/configuration compatibility. The selected reference build and all runtime evidence must still be identified separately.
